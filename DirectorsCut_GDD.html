01 / VISION

A film set becomes a timed route puzzle

You are a director operating the camera on a stylized classic-horror soundstage. Before a take, explore the set, learn the actors’ cues, and mark your route. Once you call “Action!”, the cast and environment perform a fixed sequence. Reach shooting positions and frame the action before each moment disappears.

The studio needs one essential shot to approve the scene. Optional footage reveals additional character, setup, and aftermath: your Director’s Cut. A safe route delivers the assignment; a demanding route creates a richer version of the story.

Plan with knowledge

Shot windows and target positions are visible before the take. Failure should teach a better route, not reveal a hidden rule.

Execute under pressure

Movement, timing, and framing compete for attention. The scene continues even when the camera misses it.

Choose your intention

Bonus shots add narrative meaning. Success is possible without them; mastery captures the complete story.

Design provenance. The player-director premise, classic-horror scenes, preparation phase, spray markings, possible rails, timed shooting spots, required and bonus footage, and predetermined performances come from the supplied concept. First-person controls, one-set scope, all values, scoring, shot timings, and architecture below are proposed implementation defaults. “Director’s Cut” is the game’s creative interpretation of the supplied theme; an extended runtime alone is not its definition.

Reference: Teardown inspires the preparation → committed timed execution rhythm. Destruction, theft, and simulation-heavy physics are outside this design.

Session target: 5–10 minutes to learn, clear, and improve the first scene. A take lasts 45 seconds; preparation is untimed. The MVP ends with a completion screen after an approved take and offers replay. A “next scene” button appears only when another playable scene exists.

Platform defaults: desktop Windows, keyboard and mouse, Unreal Engine version already installed and supported by the team’s MCP connection. Do not change engine versions just for this document. Target 60 FPS at 1080p on the team’s declared test PC; record its hardware when testing.

03 / GAME LOOP

Prepare → perform → review → improve

Briefing → Preparation → Countdown → Filming → Results ↻

  1. Briefing: read the required beat, optional story beats, and their time windows. A short instruction explains that position alone does not capture a shot.
  2. Preparation: walk the entire set with the cast at starting marks. Inspect shot markers and the timeline. Place or remove up to 24 floor spray markers. No score or clock pressure.
  3. Countdown: interact with the clapperboard. Return the player to the fixed starting mark, lock route editing, and display a three-second countdown. Explicitly show this start position during preparation.
  4. Filming: start the shared scene clock. Actors, props, lighting cues, shot windows, and HUD all use this clock. Move between positions and hold Record while aiming correctly.
  5. Results: finish at 45 seconds, or end early if a mandatory window closes uncaptured. Show approval status, footage captured, missed-shot reasons, score, and retry.
  6. Retry: reset actors, clock, props, effects, shot progress, and player position. Preserve floor markers and optional rail configuration. Return to preparation without reloading the map.

Pause: freezes the scene clock, actor performance, and capture accumulation together. Restart during a take returns to preparation and discards that attempt. No lives, retry cost, or permanent failure.

Movement defaults: walk 450 cm/s, sprint 700 cm/s, no stamina, no required jumping. Fix the recording camera’s horizontal field of view at 75° for predictable scoring. Mouse sensitivity is adjustable; disable head bob and motion blur by default.

Optional dolly rail

In preparation, choose one of two authored, collision-checked rail routes connecting bonus positions. During filming, mount at an endpoint and ride at 900 cm/s; aiming remains manual and recording is allowed. Dismount at endpoints only. No placement through scenery, moving actors, or mandatory capture zones. A rail improves the route but is never required for base completion. Freeform spline editing is deferred.

04 / CAPTURE CONTRACT

Be there, frame it, hold the shot

A shot captures only when all conditions hold continuously for its required duration. Entering the marker is necessary but insufficient.

  1. The game is in Filming, and scene time is inside the shot’s inclusive start/end window.
  2. The player’s ground-projected position is inside the assigned capture volume: initial default radius 150 cm with 200 cm vertical tolerance.
  3. Record is held and sprint is inactive.
  4. Every required framing anchor projects inside the central 70% of viewport width and height, is in front of the camera, and is between that shot’s minimum and maximum distance.
  5. Each required anchor has a clear visibility trace from the camera. Ignore the player and cosmetic effects; the intended target actor is an acceptable trace hit. Walls, set dressing, and other actors can block the shot.

If any condition fails before capture, reset that shot’s continuous-hold timer to zero and show the highest-priority reason: wrong time → wrong position → not recording/sprinting → framing/distance → blocked. Completed shots stay captured and cannot award points twice. Evaluate every frame using scene-time delta; a paused frame adds nothing. Clamp accumulation to the portion of the frame inside the shot window, process capture first, then process window closure.

Each shot has explicit framing anchors on its subjects. The bite requires both Dracula’s head and the victim’s head; single-subject bonus shots use one head or prop anchor. These anchors and distance ranges approximate composition for the MVP. They do not claim to judge cinematic quality or the visibility of an entire character.

For each uncaptured shot:
  eligibleDelta = overlap(previousSceneTime, currentSceneTime, windowStart, windowEnd)
  if all capture conditions pass and eligibleDelta > 0:
    holdSeconds += eligibleDelta
  else:
    holdSeconds = 0
  if holdSeconds >= requiredHoldSeconds:
    mark Captured; emit ShotCaptured exactly once
After evaluation:
  if mandatory window has ended and shot is not Captured: end take as Failed
  else if sceneTime >= takeDuration: evaluate required shots; show Results

Forgiveness: begin with generous capture volumes and long shot windows. Tune position, framing, and timing separately. Avoid invisible accuracy bonuses, RNG, or penalties for aim motion.

05 / PLAYABLE SCENE

Dracula: The Midnight Visit

A theatrical castle bedroom sits inside a visible soundstage. Dracula enters, approaches a waiting victim, bites, and retreats. Optional footage turns the basic monster attack into a story of anticipation and remorse. Use original character designs and staging rather than reproducing a specific film adaptation.

Layout: a roughly 24 × 18 m navigable floor with an entry arch, bed, side mirror, curtained corridor, and backstage shortcuts. Low props shape the route without trapping the player. The actors’ blocking is separated from the main walking corridor. Actor collision must not push or snag the camera operator.

Authored performance

0–6 s: entrance and establishing motion. 6–12 s: letter reveal held clearly. 12–16 s: approach. 16–22 s: hesitation pose. 22–26 s: move to victim. 26–33 s: stylized bite pose with minimal motion. 33–37 s: retreat. 37–43 s: look back. 43–45 s: final hold and “Cut!” The target pose remains readable throughout its advertised window.

Route feasibility

Block out approximately 8 m from start to B01, 10 m B01→B02, 12 m B02→M01, and 10 m M01→B03, measured along walkable paths. At 700 cm/s, the longest transit is about 1.7 seconds before turns and framing. Place each shot’s capture mark so the complete bonus route has at least two seconds of spare arrival time per transition in a human test. A direct start→M01 route must be obvious and safe. These distances are blockout targets, not verified map measurements.

Tutorial: first show the essential bite marker, then explain the optional shots. The first attempt can ignore every bonus. During preparation, show a static cue card at each mark with subject, window, and an example framing diagram; animated rehearsal is optional.

06 / SUCCESS & REPLAY

Approval requires every mandatory shot. Bonus footage never substitutes for the bite. On failure, show captured bonus shots for feedback, but the attempt has no submitted score and does not update the best score.

07 / PRESENTATION

Readable filmmaking, theatrical horror

Art: stylized period horror, strong silhouettes, warm practical lamps against cool moonlight, visible flats and studio equipment at the set edges. Use restrained bloodless staging for the bite. Optional footage should feel like intentional storytelling, not collectible hunting.

HUD: phase label, large take clock, required-shot status, upcoming shot countdown, and a compact timeline. The active shot displays a framing bracket, hold-progress ring, and specific invalid-condition text. Gold means mandatory, cyan means optional; icons and words repeat the distinction. Captured markers become checkmarks. Floor spray is visible from the player view but never obscures subjects.

Audio: clapperboard, “Action!”, timed actor cues, a recording hum, capture chime, last-five-seconds cue, and “Cut!”. Subtitle all spoken cues. Use audio as reinforcement, never the sole way to detect a shot window.

Accessibility: adjustable sensitivity, high-contrast text, no required camera shake, no flashing failure effect, pause, and repeatable attempts. Minimum 18 px-equivalent HUD text at 1080p. Keep default route markers distinct from interactive shot markers.

Minimum assets: two actors; a castle-room modular kit; bed, letter, curtains, and arch; camera/clapperboard props or simple UI equivalents; four shot markers; one spray decal; a short performance sequence; HUD, briefing, pause, and results widgets; a small set of cue sounds. Placeholder meshes and pose holds are sufficient for initial validation.

Store definitions as a Data Asset or Data Table according to tool support. Validate on load: unique IDs, existing markers/anchors, at least one mandatory shot, 0 ≤ start < end ≤ duration, hold ≤ window length, and valid distance ranges. Invalid definitions block the take with a useful developer message.

Reset contract

Stop and seek the sequence to zero; restore recorded initial transforms and relevant prop/light state; clear latent timers, temporary effects, audio, captured flags, and evaluator progress; release sprint/record inputs; teleport the player to the start; restore input mode and HUD; preserve prep markers. Explicit reset must work even if the sequence ended normally or was stopped early. Do not depend only on automatic sequence state restoration.

10 / ACCEPTANCE TESTS

Observable behavior defines “done”

  1. Basic approval: capture M01 only. Result is Studio Cut, 1,000 points, and scene completion.
  2. Full cut: capture all four shots. Result is Director’s Cut, exactly 2,000 points; no duplicate reward after holding Record longer.
  3. Mandatory failure: miss M01 with bonuses captured. End immediately after its window closes; show Retake Required and do not update approved best score.
  4. Capture gating: test outside volume, behind camera, out of safe frame, out of distance, blocked by a wall, before/after window, and while sprinting. Each prevents capture and provides the correct reason.
  5. Continuous hold: break a valid shot halfway through. Progress resets; reentering requires the full uninterrupted hold.
  6. Timing boundaries: capture at the end of a window; award only if enough eligible hold time accumulated. Repeat at 30, 60, and 120 FPS caps; no pause or outside-window time is counted.
  7. Pause: pause mid-hold for five seconds. Actor, clock, and progress remain frozen and resume together.
  8. Reset: retry ten times, including early restart and failure. Actors, light cues, shot status, and player start match; route marks remain; no duplicated sound or callbacks.
  9. Route fairness: a tester completes both the direct mandatory route and all-bonus route on foot. Record timings and adjust any transition lacking the intended arrival margin.
  10. Packaged build: launch on the test PC without the editor. Complete, fail, retry, pause, and exit successfully; verify startup map, controls, text legibility, and performance target.
