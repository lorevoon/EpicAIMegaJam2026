Director's Cut: The Village Raid

You are directing a fantasy tragedy on a working film set. A swordsman's small kindness, a monster raid and a broken promise play out across two stages. Read the storyboard, study the rehearsal, rig the village with as many cameras as you need, then cut each take live. Capture every beat of the story, and keep the film crew out of the movie.

GDD v2.2Updated 14 Nov 2026Unreal Engine 5.8 · WindowsDeadline Mon 16 Nov 10:00 HKT
Vision

A tragedy in two takes, framed around a busy film set

The studio wants a fantasy tragedy: an act of kindness introduces a swordsman and the archer who loves him, a monster raid takes the life of the man he helped, and their return to defend the village ends with Romeo’s death and a promise Juliet must remember alone. The set is full of crew in black with headphones who keep wandering into the action. Your job is to find the angles that tell the story and hide the production. Teardown inspires the rhythm: unlimited, calm preparation, then one committed, unforgiving take where the plan meets reality.

Learn the scene

The storyboard says what happens and in what order, never where or when. The rehearsal shows you the rest, at your own pace.

Frame out the crew

Every second a crew member is visible on the live camera costs points. Houses, the mill, fences and rooftops are your best friends.

Cut it live

Only the live camera records. With beats overlapping, choosing when to cut is the core decision of the game.

Capture targets rise from 4 to 5 beats across the two stages. The mill-side bench, the old man's box and the promise under the tree are planted early so they return during the tragedy.

Camera rigs

As many cameras as you want, two kinds

Static

Starts at eye level (170 cm). WASD moves horizontally within 200 cm of its placement centre at 120 cm/s; mouse aims.

Crane

Starts 700 cm above the ground, aimed down for a bird’s-eye view. WASD moves horizontally within 350 cm of its placement centre at 180 cm/s; mouse aims all the way to straight down. A mast and boom support the camera. No rail installation is needed.

Position, angle and zoom save automatically during preparation. Press 1–9 to switch directly between cameras. During a take, each camera retains its last pose when switching away; retry restores all prepared presets. Movement stops at scenery; saving a preset does not expand the movement radius. Zoom runs from 20° to 90° horizontal field of view; zooming in extends the distance at which beats still count.

Capture rules

What counts as filming a beat, and what counts as crew in shot

A beat is captured when

  • its time window is running, its scored pose has begun (the pose gate is open) and the camera is the live one;
  • every capture anchor (head, face, prop, weapon hand, fire cue or body) sits inside the central 70% of the frame;
  • each anchor is within range (2,000 cm, more when zoomed) and not blocked by scenery;
  • face-required anchors are seen from the front; side angles are accepted for the kiss and the mourning pose;
  • all of that holds continuously for the beat's hold time (1.75–2.5 s). Cutting away resets an unfinished hold.

A crew member is in shot when

  • their head or body is anywhere in the live camera's full frame;
  • within 4,000 cm and not hidden behind scenery.

Penalty: −150 points per second, with a red frame alarm.

Crew wear black clothes and headphones so they read instantly. The goblins, orcs and armoured orc are story performers and never trigger the penalty; the penalty belongs only to the separate crew intruders, so the required raid and combat shots score normally.

Pose gates

Each scored moment is gated to its pose so the player must witness its result, and the resulting pose stays readable for the full hold: fire anchors activate after both fire cues, the old man's death after his fall, the rescue after the arrow hit, and the stabbing after the swordsman falls. The monster-hand reveal (3.3) uses a dedicated weapon-hand anchor.

Playable scene

The Village Raid: two stages, nine capture beats

The story is set in a windmill village: a lane with a mill-side bench, a storehouse and a gate, a mill crossing over the water, a short walkable ramp up to a landing, and a downhill path from the mill to a tree clearing. Stage 1 is shot on a compact 30 × 24 metre green-screen soundstage in L_DC_Act1_Promise, using selected WindmillValley practical props; Stage 2 reuses the same lane, mill crossing, ramp and tree clearing. The same couple carries the story through all two acts. Timings below are proposed rehearsal values, measured from the start of each take and hidden from the player. A capture window contains an authored sequence; its hold is enabled once the listed scored pose begins.

Staging notes

Continuity. Romeo and Juliet start at their exact final Act 1 positions beneath the tree. They witness Mr. Smith’s murder from behind him. The same lead orc kills Mr. Smith and fights Romeo; Romeo kills that orc before dying himself.

Studio and replay. Film in L_DC_Act2_Set, copied from Act 1’s studio, with minimal practical props. L_DC_Act2_Real is a separate copy of Act 1’s village dressed with large Niagara flames, smoke and roof fires. The original prop fire is removed. The shared cast and terrain preserve world alignment; replay uses the recorded lens position, rotation, FOV and scene time.

Character assets. The orc is re-imported from the complete source torso, legs, head and armour with opaque materials. Romeo uses the source sword’s original wrist bind. Five orcs form the raid troop.

Camera and scoring. Five required capture beats. Gates occur at 3, 8.5, 11, 27.5 and 34.5 seconds; holds last 1, 1, 1, 1 and 2 seconds.

Timing and sound. The take remains 38 seconds. Preserve the reaction and the final nine-second mourning passage. Gasps and sobbing are animated; no newly recorded voice lines are included.

Evaluation

Replay first, then the grade

Every beat is required. Capture all 4 or 5 beats to clear the stage and unlock the next one; there are no bonus beats.

Beat points   = 250 × beats captured             (max 1,000 / 1,250 / 1,500)
Crew cost     = 150 × seconds crew were on the live camera
Final score   = max(0, Beat points − Crew cost)   if every beat was captured
              = 0, "Retake required"              otherwise (missed beats listed)
GradeCondition
S · Director's CutAll beats captured and zero crew screen time
AScore ≥ 85% of the stage maximum
BScore ≥ 65% of the stage maximum
C · Studio CutAll beats captured; stage cleared
RetakeOne or more beats missed; the results screen names each missed beat

The replay plays back exactly what went out live: every cut, aim, zoom and dolly move, recorded 30 times a second, with letterboxing and the recording look. Crew moments flash a small red marker on the replay's timeline so players learn where they slipped.

Acceptance tests

Done means a player can do this

  1. Read the Stage 1 storyboard, rehearse at 2× and paused, and scrub to the kiss (1.4).
  2. Place one camera of each type; set a dolly's start and a crane's height; see them restored after a retake.
  3. Capture all 4 beats of The Promise: Stage 1 is cleared (at least C, Studio Cut) and The Raid unlocks.
  4. Miss one beat: "Retake required", the missed beat is named on the results screen, no best score saved.
  5. Leave a crew member on the live camera for 2 s: exactly 300 points are lost and the alarm shows. Framing the armoured orc or a goblin costs nothing.
  6. Cut away halfway through a beat: its hold resets.
  7. Frame 2.4 before the old man falls, or 3.3 without the raider's weapon hand: the hold does not start (pose gate and weapon-hand anchor work).
  8. Clear all two stages, 9 beats in total.
  9. Watch the replay: every cut and camera move matches the take.
  10. Retake ten times in a row: actors, crew, props, fire, clock and scores reset; cameras stay.
  11. The packaged build runs outside the editor at 60 FPS at 1080p and exits cleanly.
