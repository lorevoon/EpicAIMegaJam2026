The Village Raid

An act of kindness introduces a swordsman and the archer who loves him. A monster raid takes the life of the man he helped. Their return to defend the village ends with a rescue, a fatal moment of gratitude and a promise she must remember alone.

The Promise · 4The Raid · 5The Broken Promise · 6Production notes

The same couple carries the story through all three acts. Capture targets increase from 4 to 5 to 6. The mill-side bench, old man’s box and mountain promise establish the images that return during the tragedy.

Each card gives the action, dialogue, suggested composition and scored moment. Timings are proposed rehearsal values. A capture window contains an authored sequence, with its hold enabled once the listed scored pose begins. Timecodes are measured from the start of each take.

Set Mill crossing → upper landing → lane below

Production notes

  • Progression. Capture all 4, 5 or 6 beats to clear each stage. Show missed beats on the results screen. Keep production timecodes hidden in the player storyboard and discoverable through rehearsal.
  • Dramatic callbacks. Show the old man’s distinctive vest, walking stick and marked box clearly in Act 1. Use those same props and costume in Act 2. Repeat the lovers’ forehead-to-forehead composition in the ending. Carry “Promise” from the lookout into “You promised” beside his body.
  • The recognizable cliché. Let the archer’s successful arrow feel like a rescue. Give the couple a few seconds to smile before the audience sees the monster’s hand move. Keep the swordsman unaware until the stabbing. The final quiet hold gives the loss room to land.
  • Actor continuity. The blade-bearing raider kills the old man, duels the swordsman, takes the arrow and delivers the final stab. Keep its shoulder armor, weapon and fall location consistent. Two other monsters establish the troop and move into background lanes. The arrow wounds the main raider, allowing its brief recovery. Its final collapse establishes why the archer can reach her lover.
  • Geography. Keep the old man’s bench and box close to the mill crossing. Give the archer a short landing reached by a walkable ramp, with a measured descent of about five seconds. Place the dueling pair within easy view of the landing. The couple reach the lower path before the final beat of Act 2.
  • Camera and scoring. Use live-camera framing and continuous holds. A cut resets an unfinished hold. Gate each scored moment to its relevant pose so the player must witness its result. Fire anchors activate after both fire cues. The old man’s death activates after his fall. The rescue activates after the arrow hit. The stabbing activates after the swordsman falls. Leave each resulting pose readable for the full hold. The monster-hand reveal needs a dedicated weapon-hand anchor. Accept side angles for the kiss and mourning pose.
  • Difficulty. Act 1 has separated targets and broad setups. Act 2 requires cameras across the village and a personal reaction shot. Act 3 alternates between elevation, action, an ominous background detail and an intimate final composition. Paper timing permits all required holds. Rehearse actual cameras and pose gates before locking the windows.
  • Animation staging. Use a pre-attached box for the carry and hide the transfer with the old man’s grateful pat. Use offset attacks and timed reactions for combat. Animate the arrow separately and attach a lodged-arrow prop after impact. Mask the stab contact with the side angle and a sound cue. Use an aligned kneel and seated cradling pose for the final scene. Paired kiss and mourning poses need an early rig test.
  • Sound. Begin with mill water, birds and village chatter. Use the fire alarm and battle sounds during the raid. Drop the music as the old man’s hand falls. Let the thank-you play against a brief lull, then use the sword hitting the ground, the archer’s scream and restrained sobbing. End with fire and water under her final line. Subtitle every line.
  • Set reuse. Reuse the village lane, mill crossing, short upper ramp and mountain lookout. Drive spreading fire with two timed effects and a shared smoke layer. Leave bodies and damage consistent between the raid and combat.
  • Intruder penalty. Required monsters are story performers. If the existing unwanted-zombie penalty is retained, assign it to separate background intruders so the required raid and combat shots can be scored normally.
