Director’s Cut · Revised production storyboard · 9 capture beats

The Village Raid

An act of kindness introduces a swordsman and the archer who loves him. A monster raid takes the life of the man he helped. Their return to defend the village ends with Romeo’s death and a promise Juliet must remember alone.

The Promise · 4The Raid / Broken Promise · 5Production notes

The same couple carries the story through all two acts. Capture targets increase from 4 to 5. The mill-side bench, old man’s box and promise under the tree establish the images that return during the tragedy.

Each card gives the action, dialogue, suggested composition and scored moment. Timings are proposed rehearsal values. A capture window contains an authored sequence, with its hold enabled once the listed scored pose begins. Timecodes are measured from the start of each take.

The Promise

Warmth → affection → hope

Set Practical mill → shallow studio path → practical tree

Establish his kindness before the romance. The old man’s box and the lovers’ promise become visual callbacks in the following acts.

One continuous 38-second finale: an orc raid through fire → Mr. Smith’s murder → the lovers’ shock → a fatal victory → Juliet’s grief.

A fatal victory

Romeo strikes and the orc guards. The orc strikes and Romeo guards. Both strike together at 25 seconds and fall together at 26. Juliet watches in terror.

Camera
Side medium wide keeps both attacks and both deaths readable.
Capture anchors
Romeo + lead orc
Scored moment

You promised

Romeo makes the supplied Laying Hand Gesture in his final moments. Juliet approaches, kneels and plays the supplied Holding Down motion over him as the village burns.

Camera

Scored moment

Production notes

Act 1 practical film set. A 56 × 48 metre enclosed studio accommodates the complete user-added mill group at original scale: the large house, windmill, smaller house, wheat cart, wheat stacks, barrels and mailbox. Four green-screen walls replace the distant environment. All floors are neutral charcoal or earth; overhead soft lights illuminate the enclosed set. Gentle access ramps connect raised scenery to the walking lanes. The old outdoor coordinates are reference landmarks only; the compact studio now uses local marks near the origin. Romeo helps Mr. Smith near (1100, 300, 62), then walks with Juliet down a short, clear path to the tree near (1150, -600, 12), in Unreal centimetres. Separated approach lanes keep the cast clear of each other and the scenery. Crew use designated clear lanes and the access ramp. Space jumps while the player is on foot; it retains playback control during review.

Juliet walking animation. The existing walk remains active. The requested Mixamo Female Walk download and retargeting remain deferred. Dialogue is recorded in the GDD and level beat captions; the scene remains unvoiced.

Studio timing and production dressing. Four capture beats in a 20-second take, with 1-second capture holds. Blocking, animation cues and the box set-down event are retimed together; the shared walk lasts from 7 to approximately 12 seconds over about 9 metres, with normal-speed animation clips. Dialogue remains the full script reference; this fast visual take is unvoiced. Exposed lighting bars, softboxes, stands, a boom microphone, dolly rails, equipment cases and a monitor table establish a working film set. Leave a clear camera lane across the front. Replay uses a matched outdoor version. L_DC_Act1_Promise holds the shared cast, waypoints, slopes, tree, mill group and story beats. L_DC_Act1_Set holds studio walls, floor, lighting and equipment; L_DC_Act1_Real holds the outdoor ground, trees, distant ridges, sky and daylight. Both environment layers use the identity transform. Preparation and filming show the set; review shows the real environment and hides crew, clapperboard and all camera rails. Returning to preparation restores them. The existing 20-second action and four capture beats are unchanged. Each recorded frame preserves lens position, rotation, FOV, camera slot and scene time; cuts and recording pauses remain hard cuts in the finished film. Shared action geometry is edited once, so there is no duplicate slope or actor route to drift out of alignment.

  • Continuity. Romeo and Juliet start at their exact final Act 1 positions beneath the tree. They witness Mr. Smith’s murder from behind him. The same lead orc kills Mr. Smith and fights Romeo; Romeo kills that orc before dying himself.
  • Studio and replay. Film in L_DC_Act2_Set, copied from Act 1’s studio, with minimal practical props. L_DC_Act2_Real is a separate copy of Act 1’s village dressed with large Niagara flames, smoke and roof fires. The original prop fire is removed. The shared cast and terrain preserve world alignment; replay uses the recorded lens position, rotation, FOV and scene time.
  • Character assets. The orc is re-imported from the complete source torso, legs, head and armour with opaque materials. Romeo uses the source sword’s original wrist bind. Five orcs form the raid troop.
  • Camera and scoring. Five required capture beats, filmed from six distinct reference cameras: entrance, Smith's death, reaction/run, two close combat angles, and farewell. Gates occur at 3, 8.5, 11, 27.5 and 31 seconds; holds last 1, 1, 1, 1 and 2 seconds.
  • Timing and sound. The take and reference cut remain 38 seconds. Brief slow motion emphasizes Smith's fall and the fatal exchange. Supplied voices, fire, music, growls and sword impacts follow scene time. Romeo says NOO!, then Run! Juliet!, immediately followed by Juliet's refusal. The final whispered promise line is removed; the farewell and sobbing remain.
